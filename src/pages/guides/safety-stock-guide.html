Safety stock is buffer inventory that protects you from stockouts when demand spikes or a supplier delivers late. Too little and you lose sales; too much and you tie up cash and warehouse space. The right amount is a calculation, not a gut feeling.

Three formulas, from simple to robust

MethodFormulaUse when
Rule of thumb(Max daily sales × max lead time) − (avg daily sales × avg lead time)No statistics available
Demand variabilityZ × σd × √LTLead time is reliable
Demand + lead-time variabilityZ × √(LT × σd² + d² × σLT²)Both vary (most imports)

Z is the service-level factor, σd the standard deviation of daily demand, LT the average lead time in days, d the average daily demand and σLT the standard deviation of lead time.

Choosing a service level

Service levelZ-scoreTypical use
90%1.28C items, low-margin, easily substituted
95%1.65Most B items — a common default
98%2.05A items, key customers
99%2.33Critical parts, line-stopping components

Service level costs rise steeply: going from 95% to 99% raises safety stock by about 41% (2.33 ÷ 1.65). Segment with ABC analysis rather than applying 99% everywhere.

Worked example

Average daily demand 120 units (σ = 30), average lead time 14 days (σ = 3), target 95%.

  • Demand term: 14 × 30² = 12,600
  • Lead-time term: 120² × 3² = 129,600
  • Safety stock = 1.65 × √142,200 ≈ 1.65 × 377 ≈ 622 units
  • Reorder point = 120 × 14 + 622 = 2,302 units

Notice that lead-time variability contributes about 10× more than demand variability here. Fixing supplier reliability is often cheaper than holding more stock. Try it: safety stock calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Using monthly σ with daily lead times — keep units consistent.
  • Ignoring lead-time variability for overseas suppliers.
  • Never recalculating — review at least quarterly or after demand shifts.
  • Counting safety stock as "excess" inventory in cost-cutting drives.

Reduce safety stock without raising risk

  1. Shorten and stabilize lead times (nearshoring, dual sourcing — see nearshoring guide).
  2. Improve forecast accuracy to shrink σd.
  3. Pool inventory in fewer locations.
  4. Use vendor-managed inventory with key suppliers.
