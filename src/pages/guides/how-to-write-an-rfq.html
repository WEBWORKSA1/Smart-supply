A Request for Quotation (RFQ) asks suppliers to price a clearly defined product or service. When the spec is vague, suppliers either ignore the request or pad the price to cover unknowns. A tight RFQ gets you more responses, comparable prices and fewer surprises after the purchase order.

RFI vs RFP vs RFQ

DocumentUse whenYou receive
RFIYou're exploring the market and qualifying suppliersCapabilities, capacity, certifications
RFPThe solution is open — you want suppliers to propose howApproach + pricing + timeline
RFQYou know exactly what you needPrice, MOQ, lead time, terms

The 9 fields every RFQ needs

  1. Product description & specifications — materials, dimensions, tolerances, colors, finishes, drawings or reference samples.
  2. Quantity and unit — plus price breaks you want quoted (e.g., 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000).
  3. Quality standards — required certifications (ISO 9001, CE, FDA, FSC) and inspection method (e.g., AQL 2.5).
  4. Packaging & labeling — retail packaging, carton marks, barcodes, pallet requirements.
  5. Incoterm and named place — e.g., FCA Ningbo or DAP Toronto. See our Incoterms guide.
  6. Delivery date — required ship or arrival date, and whether partial shipments are allowed.
  7. Payment terms — e.g., 30% deposit / 70% against B/L copy, or net 30.
  8. Quote validity & currency — "Quote in USD, valid 60 days".
  9. Response deadline & format — a date and a table format so quotes line up.

Copy-paste RFQ template

Subject: RFQ – [Product] – [Quantity] – Reply by [Date]

We are sourcing [product] for [market/use]. Please quote for:
• Specs: [materials, size, finish; drawing attached]
• Quantities: [1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 pcs]
• Certifications: [ISO 9001, …]
• Packaging: [details]
• Terms: [Incoterm + place], [payment terms]
• Required ship date: [date]

Please include unit price per tier, MOQ, tooling/sample costs, production lead time, and quote validity. Reply by [date].

Mistakes that kill response rates

  • No quantity — suppliers can't price without volume; many won't reply.
  • Unrealistic target price with no justification — share a range or benchmark instead.
  • Sending to 50 suppliers — quality suppliers de-prioritize "spray" RFQs. 3–6 well-matched suppliers is the sweet spot.
  • Hidden requirements revealed after quoting (certifications, special packaging) — forces requotes and erodes trust.

Comparing quotes fairly

Normalize every quote to the same basis before choosing:

  • Convert to one Incoterm and currency.
  • Add tooling, samples, packaging upgrades and payment-term costs.
  • Calculate landed cost per unit including freight, duty and fees.
  • Score non-price factors with a weighted supplier scorecard.
The cheapest unit price rarely means the lowest total cost of ownership. Lead time, defect rate and communication quality routinely outweigh a 5% price gap.
