Most sourcing disasters — wrong specs, missed deadlines, outright fraud — are preventable with a structured vetting process. Use this checklist before you send a deposit to any new supplier.

The 10-point checklist

  1. Legal existence. Verify business registration in the official company registry of the supplier's country. Check that the name matches the bank account beneficiary.
  2. Manufacturer or trader? Ask for the business license scope, factory address and photos/videos of production lines. Traders aren't bad — but you should know who makes your product.
  3. Certifications. Request certificates (ISO 9001, BSCI, FSC, CE test reports) and verify numbers with the issuing body's online database.
  4. Factory audit. Commission a third-party audit or do a video walkthrough covering capacity, equipment, QC process and working conditions.
  5. References. Ask for 2–3 export customers in your region and actually call them.
  6. Samples. Pay for pre-production samples; approve a "golden sample" that becomes the quality reference.
  7. Communication test. Response speed, technical understanding and English (or your language) proficiency predict the entire relationship.
  8. Financial health. Credit reports from providers such as Dun & Bradstreet, or bank references for large orders.
  9. Compliance. Screen against sanctions lists and forced-labor risk lists relevant to your market.
  10. Contract & payment terms. Written PO with specs, Incoterm, delivery date, penalties, IP clauses and inspection rights.

Red flags

  • Bank account in a different company name or country than the supplier.
  • Last-minute "our bank details changed" emails — call to verify on a known number.
  • Prices far below every other quote.
  • Refusal of third-party inspection or factory visits.
  • Pressure to pay 100% upfront for a first order.

Quality control stages

  • Pre-production: materials and components check.
  • During production (DUPRO): catch defects at 20–50% completion.
  • Pre-shipment inspection (PSI): AQL sampling at 80–100% packed.
  • Container loading check: quantities, packing and seal.
