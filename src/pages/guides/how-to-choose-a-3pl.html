A third-party logistics provider (3PL) stores your inventory, picks and packs orders, ships them and often handles returns. The right partner lets you scale without building warehouses; the wrong one costs you customers. Here's how to choose well.

When outsourcing makes sense

  • You ship more orders than your team can pack reliably, or volume is very seasonal.
  • You need to be closer to customers in new regions or countries.
  • Warehouse space, labour or systems are becoming your bottleneck.
  • You want to switch fixed costs (rent, staff, equipment) to variable, per-order costs.

Keep logistics in-house if fulfilment is a core differentiator that requires custom handling — or if your volume is so high that owning the operation is clearly cheaper.

How 3PL pricing works

  • Receiving: per pallet, carton, unit or hour.
  • Storage: per pallet, shelf, bin or cubic foot per month — long-term storage often costs more.
  • Pick and pack: per order plus per extra item; kitting and inserts extra.
  • Packaging materials and postage: passed through or marked up.
  • Account fees and minimums: monthly minimum billing, onboarding and integration fees.
  • Returns: per return, plus inspection and restocking.

Ask every candidate to price the same sample month — your real order profile — so you can compare total cost, not rate cards.

Questions for your 3PL RFP

  1. Which sales channels, marketplaces and ERP systems do you integrate with, and how?
  2. What are your order cut-off times and same-day ship rates?
  3. How do you measure inventory and order accuracy, and what happens when you miss?
  4. How do you handle peak season — capacity guarantees, temporary staff, surcharges?
  5. Can we see live inventory and order status in a portal or via API?
  6. What insurance covers our goods while stored, and up to what value?
  7. What are the contract term, notice period and exit process (including data and inventory transfer)?
  8. Can we visit the facility and speak to two current clients with similar products?

Red flags

  • No live system access or reports "on request" only.
  • Vague answers on accuracy measurement.
  • Long lock-in with steep exit fees.
  • Pricing that only works at your best-case volumes.
  • Reluctance to share references or allow a site visit.
