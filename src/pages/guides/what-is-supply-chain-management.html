Supply chain management (SCM) is the coordination of everything required to get a product from raw materials to the customer — and back again when it's returned. It spans planning, purchasing, production, warehousing, transportation and customer service, and connects suppliers, manufacturers, logistics providers and retailers.

The five core processes

The widely used SCOR model (maintained by ASCM) breaks the supply chain into processes. The classic five are:

ProcessWhat happens
PlanDemand forecasting, S&OP, inventory and capacity planning
SourceSupplier selection, RFQs, contracts, purchasing
MakeProduction scheduling, manufacturing, quality control
DeliverOrder management, warehousing, transportation, customs
ReturnReverse logistics, repairs, recycling

Newer SCOR versions also add Orchestrate and Enable to cover governance, data and sustainability.

KPIs that matter

  • OTIF (on-time in-full) — the customer's view of performance.
  • Inventory turnover and days of inventory — cash efficiency (calculator).
  • Forecast accuracy — drives everything downstream.
  • Cash-to-cash cycle time — days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.
  • Total landed cost and cost to serve.
  • Supplier lead time & variability.

Trends shaping 2026

  • AI agents for demand sensing, supplier discovery, procurement negotiation and exception management.
  • Tariff agility: constant re-costing of origins, HS classification reviews and duty mitigation.
  • Nearshoring & China+1 diversification (guide).
  • Scope 3 emissions reporting pushing carbon data into sourcing decisions (CO₂ calculator).
  • Traceability requirements for forced-labor and deforestation regulations.

Careers and certifications

Common roles include buyer, procurement manager, demand planner, supply planner, logistics coordinator, warehouse manager and supply-chain analyst.
