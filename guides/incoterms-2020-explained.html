Incoterms 2020 Explained: All 11 Rules, Who Pays & Where Risk Transfers

By Smart.Supply EditorialUpdated 2026-09-18⏱ 3 min read

A plain-English guide to the 11 Incoterms 2020 rules — EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP, FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF — with a comparison table and tips for choosing the right one.

Incoterms® are standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). They define three things in a sales contract: who arranges and pays for transport, who handles export and import clearance, and the exact point where risk passes from seller to buyer. The current edition is Incoterms 2020, in force since 1 January 2020.

What Incoterms don't cover: transfer of ownership (title), payment terms, or what happens if a party breaches the contract. Those belong elsewhere in your purchase agreement.

The two families of rules

The 11 rules split into two groups:

  • Any mode of transport (7 rules): EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP. Use these for containers, air, road, rail and multimodal shipments.
  • Sea and inland waterway only (4 rules): FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF. Use these for bulk or break-bulk cargo loaded directly onto a vessel.

A common mistake is using FOB or CIF for containerized cargo. Containers are usually handed over at a terminal days before loading, so the ICC recommends FCA instead of FOB and CPT/CIP instead of CFR/CIF for container freight.

What changed in Incoterms 2020

  • DAT became DPU — delivery can now be at any place, not just a terminal, with the seller unloading.
  • CIP insurance level raised to Institute Cargo Clauses (A) "all risks"; CIF stays at minimum cover (Clauses C).
  • FCA on-board bill of lading option — buyer can instruct its carrier to issue an on-board B/L to the seller, which helps with letters of credit.
  • Security-related costs and own-transport arrangements are addressed more explicitly.

The "C" rule trap: two critical points

With CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF, the seller pays freight to the destination, but risk passes at origin when goods are handed to the first carrier or loaded on the vessel. If a container is damaged at sea under CIF, it is the buyer's loss — recovered through the insurance the seller bought. Buyers often assume "seller pays freight" means "seller carries risk". It doesn't.

How to choose the right Incoterm

  1. New importer with no forwarder? DAP or DDP keeps logistics with the seller — but you pay for that convenience inside the unit price and lose visibility.
  2. Established importer with a forwarder? FCA (or FOB for bulk) gives you control of freight rates and routing, typically the lowest total landed cost.
  3. Avoid EXW for exports unless you can legally handle export clearance in the seller's country; FCA is usually the better choice.
  4. Avoid DDP as a seller unless you can register as importer of record and recover VAT/GST in the destination country.
  5. Always name a precise place: "FCA Shenzhen Yantian Terminal, Incoterms® 2020" — not just "FCA China".

FAQ

Is FOB still valid in 2026?

Yes. Incoterms 2020 remain the current ICC edition, and FOB is valid — but it is designed for sea freight where goods are loaded on board. For containers, FCA is recommended.

Do Incoterms apply to domestic sales?

They can. Incoterms 2020 explicitly may be used in both international and domestic contracts.

Can I still use Incoterms 2010?

Yes, parties may agree to any edition, but you must state it in the contract (for example "Incoterms® 2010"). Using the current edition avoids confusion.
