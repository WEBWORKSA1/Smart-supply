EOQ (Economic Order Quantity): Formula, Example & When Not to Use It

By Smart.Supply EditorialUpdated 2026-09-18⏱ 3 min read

Learn the EOQ formula, see a worked example, understand its assumptions, and learn practical adjustments for MOQs, price breaks and variable demand.

The Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is the order size that minimizes the combined cost of placing orders and holding inventory. Order too often and ordering costs pile up; order too much and cash sits on shelves. EOQ finds the balance point.

The EOQ formula

EOQ = √(2DS ÷ H)
D = annual demand (units) · S = cost per order · H = holding cost per unit per year

Ordering cost (S) includes purchasing admin, receiving, inspection and fixed freight per order. Holding cost (H) includes cost of capital, storage, insurance, shrinkage and obsolescence — commonly estimated at 20–30% of unit value per year.

Worked example

Annual demand is 12,000 units, each order costs $75 to place and receive, and holding one unit for a year costs $2.50.

  • EOQ = √(2 × 12,000 × 75 ÷ 2.5) = √720,000 ≈ 849 units
  • Orders per year = 12,000 ÷ 849 ≈ 14.1 (roughly every 26 days)
  • Annual ordering cost ≈ 14.1 × $75 = $1,060; annual holding cost ≈ 849 ÷ 2 × $2.50 = $1,061

At the EOQ, ordering and holding costs are equal — that's the signature of the optimum. Try it in the EOQ calculator.

Assumptions behind EOQ

  • Demand is constant and known.
  • Lead time is constant.
  • Price doesn't change with order size.
  • The whole order arrives at once; no stockouts.

Real supply chains break all four. That doesn't make EOQ useless — the total cost curve is flat near the optimum, so ordering 10–20% above or below EOQ usually costs very little extra.

Practical adjustments

  1. Supplier MOQ: if MOQ > EOQ, order the MOQ (or negotiate it down).
  2. Quantity discounts: compute total cost (purchase + ordering + holding) at EOQ and at each price-break quantity; pick the lowest.
  3. Container fill: round to full cartons, pallets or containers when freight is the dominant ordering cost.
  4. Variable demand: pair EOQ with safety stock and a reorder point.
  5. Seasonality: recalculate EOQ per season using that season's demand rate.

MethodBest for
