ABC Analysis: How to Classify Inventory (with a Worked Example)

By Smart.Supply EditorialUpdated 2026-11-16⏱ 3 min read

Use ABC analysis to rank inventory by annual value, set different service levels and review cycles for A, B and C items, and combine it with XYZ analysis for demand variability.

Not every SKU deserves the same attention. ABC analysis ranks items by annual consumption value so you can focus planning effort, safety stock and supplier management where the money is. It applies the Pareto principle: a small share of items usually drives most of the value.

The three classes

ClassTypical share of SKUsTypical share of valueManagement approach
A10–20%~70–80%Tight control, frequent review, high service level, strong supplier relationships
B~30%~15–25%Moderate control, periodic review
C50–60%~5–10%Simple rules, larger lot sizes, lower service level, automation

The cut-offs are conventions, not laws. Pick thresholds that make sense for your range.

How to run an ABC analysis

  1. For each SKU, calculate annual consumption value = annual units × unit cost.
  2. Sort SKUs from highest to lowest value.
  3. Calculate each SKU's share of total value and the cumulative share.
  4. Assign classes by cumulative share — for example A up to ~70–80%, B up to ~95%, C for the rest.

Worked example

Ten SKUs with a total annual consumption value of $300,000:

SKUAnnual valueCumulative %Class
1$120,00040.0%A
2$90,00070.0%A
3$30,00080.0%B
4$22,00087.3%B
5$15,00092.3%B
6$8,00095.0%C
7$6,00097.0%C
8$4,00098.3%C
9$3,00099.3%C
10$2,000100%C

Two SKUs (20% of items) hold 70% of the value. Three B items hold 22.3%, and five C items — half the range — hold just 7.7%. Your planning time should follow those numbers.

Turning classes into policies

  • Service levels: for example 98–99% for A, 95% for B, 90% for C. Calculate the buffer with the safety stock calculator.
  • Review frequency: weekly for A, monthly for B, quarterly for C.
  • Ordering: smaller, frequent orders for A to limit capital tied up; larger EOQ-based lots for C to cut admin.
  • Cycle counting: count A items monthly, B quarterly, C once or twice a year.
  • Suppliers: dual-source and negotiate hardest on A items.

Add XYZ analysis for demand variability

ABC says how valuable an item is; XYZ says how predictable its demand is, using the coefficient of variation (standard deviation ÷ mean of demand). X items are stable, Y items fluctuate, Z items are erratic. An AX item (valuable, predictable) suits lean replenishment; an AZ item (valuable, erratic) needs close collaboration with customers and suppliers; CZ items are candidates for make-to-order or discontinuation.

Common mistakes

  • Ranking by unit price instead of annual consumption value.
  • Never re-running the analysis — redo it at least every six months.
  • Ignoring critical cheap parts: a $2 component that stops a production line deserves A-level availability.
